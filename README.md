# judge-mutants

**How often does your LLM judge pass a wrong answer, or fail a right one?**

Most teams grade LLM output with another LLM: a rubric, a factuality check, a "does this match the
reference" prompt. Few measure the grader itself. judge-mutants does, the way mutation testing
measures a test suite: it takes answers you know are correct, breaks each one in a single, known
way, and asks your judge again.

- **Wrong answers** (the judge must fail them): a number moved (`30 -> 40`, `1,5 miliar -> 1,6
  miliar`), a name swapped for one of the same kind (`Denver Broncos -> Pittsburgh Steelers`), a
  unit swapped (`juta -> miliar`), a date shifted, the verb that states the fact negated, the key
  sentence dropped.
- **Controls** (the judge must still pass them): the same answer as a list, with a label in front,
  or with a harmless sentence at the end.

It reports, per kind of change, how many wrong answers the judge passed and how many controls it
failed, with the judge's own reasons. It exits with code 1 when the gate fails, so it can guard CI
when someone edits a judge prompt: a kind of change passes more often than `--max-false-pass`
(0.1), the judge passes fewer than `--min-original-pass` (0.8) of the untouched answers, more than
`--max-unreadable` (0.1) of its replies cannot be read, or nothing was measured at all. Code 2 means
bad input: a duplicate case id, a stale exclusion, a judge file with a broken rule.

```bash
judge-mutants plan --cases cases.jsonl              # what will be asked, no model calls
JM_KEY=... judge-mutants run --cases cases.jsonl --judge judges/strict.luna.json --out runs/x
```

A case is one line of JSON: `{"id", "answer", "reference", "vars": {...}, "distractors": [...]}`.
A judge is a JSON file: endpoint and model (any OpenAI-compatible API), the prompt with
`{{answer}}`, `{{reference}}` and your own slots, and how to read the verdict (a JSON path with the
values that mean pass and fail, a score threshold, or a pair of regular expressions). A reply the
rule cannot read is counted as unreadable, never guessed: a label on neither list, two JSON objects
that disagree, both regular expressions matching, or a reply cut off at the token limit.

## Method, and why the numbers can be trusted

- A change counts only for cases where the judge passed the untouched answer: failing a wrong
  answer proves nothing if the judge also fails the right one.
- Every wrong answer was read by hand before any judge ran. Where a change did not touch the fact
  the question asks about, the operator was fixed, or the change was excluded with a written
  reason (`data/*/excluded.json`). The dataset, the exclusions and the judge prompts were committed
  and tagged (`v0.1-before-runs`) before the first full run.
- Judge prompts are copied verbatim from the tools teams use (promptfoo's `llm-rubric` and
  `factuality`, OpenAI's closed-QA prompt as shipped in promptfoo; MIT), plus one strict prompt of
  my own for comparison.
- Every reply is stored next to its verdict (`runs/*/judged.jsonl`), so the published results can
  be re-scored without calling a model; a test re-reads all of them on every commit. Locally,
  complete replies are also cached, written once and atomically, so a rerun never pays twice.

## Results, 14 Oct 2026

43 SQuAD answers, 39 wrong versions of them (my rules found a fact to change in 31 answers, and two
different changes in 8) and 86 harmless edits, graded by six
judge prompts on GPT-6 Luna and Gemini 3.8 Flash; plus 12 WhatsApp drafts from
[wa-lead-extractor](https://github.com/FikriAlfaraby/wa-lead-extractor) graded by that project's
own judge. Every table and every judge reason: [`docs/results.md`](docs/results.md), generated by
`node scripts/results.ts`. The page: [fikrialfaraby.com/judges](https://fikrialfaraby.com/judges/).

- **Given the correct answer, no judge passed a wrong answer.** promptfoo's llm-rubric and
  factuality, OpenAI's closed-QA and my strict prompt, on both models: 0 of 306 verdicts.
- **Without it, the prompt decides.** promptfoo's llm-rubric with the rubric "The output correctly
  answers this question" passed 7 of 36 wrong answers on Luna and 2 of 36 on Gemini. Its reasons
  show what it checked: "The output states that Harvard was named for Increase Mather, directly
  answering the question." A strict prompt without the key passed 1 of 30 on Luna (it
  misremembers Tesla's birth year) and 0 of 36 on Gemini, but failed 7 of 43 correct answers on
  Luna.
- **Repeated three times**, the no-key rubric on Luna changed its verdict on 9 of 168 answers;
  3 wrong answers passed every time and 5 only sometimes.
- **wa-lead-extractor's judge**, which sees the lead's facts, passed 0 of 32 wrong drafts and 24 of
  24 harmless edits on both models.
- **promptfoo's verdict parsing** (0.124.1) read 4 of 9 hand-written replies the wrong way round:
  factuality's fallback takes "(wrong name)" as category E, a pass, and closed-QA passes any reply
  ending in "Y", such as "UNSATISFACTORY". Re-reading all 1,008 real replies from these runs with
  promptfoo's own code (`experiments/promptfoo`) misread none, so on these models it is a latent bug.

Known defect in the data: one SQuAD answer (Project Mercury) was cut mid-sentence when the set was
built; it is kept as run and noted in `data/squad/README.md`.

## Related work

Perturbation-based meta-evaluation of LLM judges is an active research area: Eval-Pair Matrix
(entity substitution, numerical shift, negation on grounded RAG), RAND's judge-reliability harness,
the FBI benchmark, and "Judging Against the Reference" (entity swaps in QA judging). muteval
mutates the system under test rather than the judge's answers. judge-mutants is the practical
version for a product team: your judge prompt, your model, your cases, a CI exit code.
